# Code of Conduct

## Our pledge

We pledge to make participation in OpenReady welcoming, respectful, accessible, and constructive for everyone, regardless of experience level, background, identity, ability, location, or contribution type.

Participation in OpenReady is a privilege, not a right to demand labor, access, agreement, or implementation.

## Scope

This Code of Conduct applies in all OpenReady project spaces, including:

- the repository
- issues and pull requests
- GitHub Discussions
- release discussions
- project documentation
- project-controlled email or private reporting channels
- events, calls, or community spaces represented as part of OpenReady
- public conduct by a project representative when that conduct materially affects the safety or trust of the OpenReady community

It applies to users, contributors, regular contributors, maintainers, reviewers, moderators, invited participants, and project representatives.

## Expected behavior

Participants are expected to:

- use welcoming and inclusive language
- respect different viewpoints, identities, needs, and experiences
- give and receive constructive feedback gracefully
- focus discussion on the project and the people affected by decisions
- show empathy toward other community members, especially beginners
- respect accessibility needs and communication differences
- respect privacy and avoid requesting unnecessary personal information
- accept project-scope, capacity, and final-decision boundaries
- disclose relevant conflicts of interest
- follow security and sensitive-reporting instructions
- help repair harm when a mistake is identified

## Unacceptable behavior

Unacceptable behavior includes:

- harassment, intimidation, stalking, or discriminatory language
- personal attacks, insults, threats, or trolling
- public or private abuse
- sexualized language, imagery, or unwanted attention
- deliberate misgendering or disrespect for a person's stated identity
- mocking disability, assistive-technology use, language proficiency, or beginner status
- publishing or threatening to publish another person's private information without permission
- sharing credentials, private reports, medical information, or confidential project data
- pressuring someone to disclose personal circumstances or remain constantly available
- repeated demands after a clear scope, capacity, or final-decision boundary
- retaliation against a reporter, witness, reviewer, or person who assists an investigation
- spam, repeated off-topic promotion, commercial solicitation, or attempts to use OpenReady spaces as a sales channel
- impersonating a maintainer or making unauthorized promises on behalf of the project
- knowingly submitting false allegations or fabricated evidence

A report that cannot be substantiated is not automatically a false report. Good-faith mistakes, incomplete information, and differing recollections should be handled carefully.

Use a subject such as `OpenReady Code of Conduct report`.

A useful report may include:

- what happened
- where and approximately when it happened
- who was involved or witnessed the event
- links, screenshots, or other evidence that can be shared safely
- immediate safety or access concerns
- whether the reporter wants follow-up or confidentiality within practical limits
- any conflict of interest the reviewer should know about

Do not send passwords, access tokens, unrelated private data, or evidence obtained unlawfully.

## Reports involving the current maintainer

The current maintainer is the only published Code of Conduct contact. This creates a real conflict when a report concerns that maintainer.

No independent contact will be invented or named without explicit consent.

When a report concerns the current maintainer:

1. The report should state the conflict clearly.
2. The maintainer should avoid being the sole decision-maker and should preserve the report without public disclosure.
3. When practical, the project should seek a trusted independent reviewer acceptable to the reporter and willing to protect confidentiality.
4. The reviewer should receive only the information needed for the case.
5. If no independent reviewer can be found, the project must disclose that limitation to the reporter rather than pretending that an independent process exists.
6. Platform-policy violations may also be reported through the relevant hosting or communication platform's safety or abuse process.

Immediate action may still be taken to preserve evidence, stop ongoing harm, or restrict compromised access. Such action should be narrow and reviewed later.

## Acknowledgement and response

The project aims to acknowledge a clear conduct report within five business days, subject to maintainer or reviewer availability. This is a best-effort target, not a guaranteed service level.

An acknowledgement should:

- confirm receipt
- identify who will review the report, when possible
- explain any conflict or capacity limitation
- request only necessary clarification
- avoid promising a particular outcome before review

Updates should be provided when practical, especially when review is delayed or immediate safety measures are taken.

## Privacy and evidence handling

Reports should be shared only with people who need the information to assess safety, evidence, conflicts, or enforcement.

Reviewers should:

- minimize copied personal information
- avoid placing private evidence in public issues, pull requests, commits, or workflow logs
- distinguish confirmed facts, disputed claims, and uncertainty
- allow relevant people a reasonable opportunity to respond when safe and appropriate
- consider power differences, accessibility needs, and risks of retaliation
- retain information only as long as needed for the project response and reasonable recordkeeping

Complete confidentiality cannot be guaranteed when disclosure is needed to prevent harm, follow law, protect accounts, or provide a fair response. Any necessary disclosure should be limited and explained when possible.

## Enforcement principles

Enforcement should be proportionate, consistent, conflict-aware, and focused on community safety rather than public punishment.

Relevant factors may include:

- severity and immediacy of harm
- whether behavior was repeated
- whether the person understood the boundary
- prior warnings or patterns
- power or access held by the person
- willingness to stop, repair harm, and follow conditions
- risk to reporters, witnesses, users, or project integrity
- whether a less restrictive response can reasonably protect the community

## Enforcement levels

Depending on the circumstances, the project may use one or more of the following:

### 1. Informal correction

A private or public reminder explaining the expected behavior and requesting a change.

Appropriate for a low-impact first incident when no immediate safety risk exists.

### 2. Formal warning

A documented warning that identifies the behavior, required correction, and consequence of repetition.

### 3. Content or access restriction

Removal or editing of harmful content, locking a conversation, limiting contact, restricting repository permissions, or temporarily excluding someone from project spaces.

### 4. Temporary suspension

A defined or reviewable period during which participation or access is suspended. Conditions for return may be stated.

### 5. Permanent removal

Permanent exclusion from project spaces or revocation of repository access when serious or repeated behavior makes continued participation unsafe or incompatible with the project.

### 6. Platform or service escalation

Reports may be referred to GitHub, Netlify, an email provider, or another relevant platform when platform rules, account compromise, or immediate safety concerns are involved.

Emergency restrictions may be applied before a complete review when necessary to stop ongoing harm. They should be revisited after the immediate risk has passed.

## Communicating outcomes

The reporter should receive a private outcome summary when contact remains available and sharing it is safe.

A person subject to action should normally receive:

- the behavior or policy area involved
- the restriction or required correction
- the duration or review condition, when applicable
- how to request reconsideration

Public statements should disclose only what the community needs for safety, clarity, or accountability. They should avoid unnecessary personal details, private evidence, speculation, or humiliation.

## Review and reconsideration

A person subject to a significant restriction may request one reconsideration by providing:

- a clear reason the outcome was mistaken or disproportionate
- meaningful new evidence
- evidence that required conditions were met
- a practical safety or accessibility concern with the process

A request does not automatically pause an immediate safety restriction.

When practical, reconsideration should involve someone who was not the sole original decision-maker. If independent review is unavailable, that limitation should be stated honestly.

Repeated requests without new information may be closed.

## Anti-retaliation

Retaliation against a good-faith reporter, witness, reviewer, or person who assists with safety measures is prohibited.

Retaliation may itself result in enforcement, even when the original report remains unresolved or unsubstantiated.

## False or bad-faith reports

Knowingly fabricated reports, manipulated evidence, or reports used primarily to harass or retaliate may result in enforcement.

A mistaken report, an unproven report, an accessibility-related misunderstanding, or a report made with incomplete information should not be treated as malicious without evidence of bad faith.

## Maintainer accountability

Maintainers and people with repository access are subject to the same standards and may face permission reduction or removal.

Governance, conflicts, role changes, temporary absence, succession, and emergency authority are documented in [GOVERNANCE.md](GOVERNANCE.md).
